    Model assumptions & research comparisons

    What this model does

    A shared strategy solver uses the selected associative, ACT-R-inspired, or neural memory model. It chooses available solutions by estimated effort, occasionally explores, and can make counting slips. Forgetting weakens facts and rules. Starting draw-and-count abilities remain available.

    Comparison ability controls noticing eligible example pairs and considering analogous solutions. Lesson understanding is immediate. Working-memory limits, learned action speeds, population fitting, and open-ended rule generation are future extensions.

    Cost is not response time

    The curve measures weighted solving actions. Feedback recounting and post-answer comparisons have separate costs in the log and export. Neural training updates, strategy selection, and memory indexing are not charged; these are modeling assumptions, not measured child effort.

    Compare measurements, not just curves

    Every 50 practice attempts, a non-training assessment tests all 132 eligible exercises (66 per operation). Its random stream is separate. It includes practiced facts; it is not a held-out transfer test.

    Keep a baseline to compare settings on the fixed assessment. Export includes every answer, strategy sequence, action counts, costs, rule evidence, and teaching event. Formal fitting to child data is not yet implemented.

    Research starting points

    • Siegler & Shrager (1984) · strategy choice and retrieval confidence.
    • Geary et al. (1991) · changes in strategy use and execution speed.
    • Geary et al. (2012) · working memory and addition strategies.
